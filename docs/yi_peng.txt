YI PENG FESTIVAL (Lanna Lantern Festival) | ประเพณียี่เป็ง โคมลอย โคมไฟ ล้านนา

Overview
Yi Peng is a festival of the Lanna (northern Thai) region, especially famous in Chiang Mai. "Yi" means "two" and "Peng" means "full moon" in the northern Thai language. So Yi Peng means "the full moon of the second month" in the Lanna calendar.

Dates
The Lanna calendar is about two months ahead of the central Thai lunar calendar. Because of this, the 2nd Lanna month full moon is the same night as the 12th month full moon in the central Thai calendar. This is why Yi Peng and Loy Krathong are celebrated at the same time, usually in November.

Rituals and customs
- Khom loi: sky lanterns made of thin paper (sa paper) with a fire source. People release them into the sky to send away bad luck and to make merit or a wish. Some people believe they are an offering to the Phra Ket Kaew Chulamanee pagoda in heaven, where the Buddha's hair relic is kept.
- Khom fai / khom khwaen: colorful hanging lanterns used to decorate houses, temples and streets.
- People decorate their homes and temple gates with "pratu pa" (gates made of banana trees, sugar cane and coconut leaves).
- Merit-making and listening to sermons at temples, such as the "Tang Tham Luang" (long sermons) tradition.
- A large mass lantern release is often held at Mae Jo University area (Chiang Mai), sometimes as a ticketed event.

Safety and rules
Sky lanterns can cause fires and are dangerous for airplanes. Chiang Mai authorities control when and where sky lanterns can be released, and flights at Chiang Mai International Airport are sometimes changed or cancelled during the festival nights.
