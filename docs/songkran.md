# Songkran (สงกรานต์)

Songkran is the traditional Thai New Year festival, celebrated every year from 13 to 15 April (13-15 เมษายน). The three days are called Maha Songkran (13 April), Wan Nao (14 April) and Wan Thaloeng Sok (15 April).

## Origin of the name
The word "Songkran" comes from the Sanskrit word "samkranti", meaning the astrological passage or transit of the sun from one zodiac sign to another. The Thai New Year marks the sun's movement into Aries.

## Rituals
- Rod nam dam hua (รดน้ำดำหัว): gently pouring scented water over the hands of elders to show respect and ask for their blessing.
- Making merit: people visit temples, offer food to monks, and bathe Buddha images.
- Building sand pagodas (ก่อเจดีย์ทราย): people carry sand to the temple to build small pagodas.
- Releasing fish and birds as acts of merit.
- Splashing water in the streets symbolizes washing away bad luck and the old year.

## Regional differences
In Chiang Mai, Songkran is known as Pi Mai Mueang (ปีใหม่เมือง) and is celebrated with Lanna customs. The festival lasts longer than in Bangkok, often five or more days. Highlights include a grand parade of Buddha images, sand pagoda building at temples, and respectful water ceremonies for elders. In Bangkok, celebrations are shorter and centered on water play at Silom and Khao San Road.
