LOY KRATHONG FESTIVAL | ประเพณีลอยกระทง วันเพ็ญเดือนสิบสอง

Overview
Loy Krathong is a festival where people float a small decorated basket ("krathong") on a river, canal or lake. "Loy" means "to float" and "krathong" is the floating basket.

Dates
Loy Krathong is held on the full moon night of the 12th month of the traditional Thai lunar calendar. This usually falls in November in the Western calendar. The exact date changes every year because it follows the moon, so people should check the official calendar for each year. It is not a fixed public holiday.

History and legend
- People float krathong to thank and pay respect to Phra Mae Khongkha, the goddess of water, and to ask forgiveness for using and polluting the water.
- Floating the krathong is also a symbol of letting go of anger, bad luck and bad feelings. Many people put a small piece of hair, fingernail or a coin in the krathong.
- Some people also connect the festival to Buddhist beliefs, such as paying respect to the Buddha's footprint on the bank of the Narmada River.
- A popular story says Loy Krathong started in the Sukhothai Kingdom, when Nang Noppamas (Thao Sri Chulalak), a consort of King Ramkhamhaeng (or King Li Thai in some versions), made the first lotus-shaped krathong. However, many historians believe this story was written much later, in the early Rattanakosin period (around the reign of King Rama III), so it may not be historically accurate.
- Today the Sukhothai Historical Park holds one of the biggest Loy Krathong celebrations, with light and sound shows.

Rituals and customs
- A traditional krathong is made from a slice of banana tree trunk, decorated with folded banana leaves, flowers, three incense sticks and a candle.
- People light the candle and incense, make a wish, and gently float the krathong on the water.
- Couples often float a krathong together; it is believed this will keep them together.
- There are Nang Noppamas beauty contests, krathong-making contests, fireworks and temple fairs.
- Environment: Styrofoam krathong cause pollution. Many cities now ask people to use natural materials such as banana trunk, bread or ice-cream-cone style krathong that fish can eat, or to share one krathong per family.

Regional differences
- North (Chiang Mai and Lanna region): Loy Krathong happens together with Yi Peng, the Lanna festival of the full moon of the 2nd Lanna month. People release sky lanterns (khom loi) and hang lanterns (khom fai / khom khwaen) at homes and temples. See the Yi Peng document.
- Tak: "Loy Krathong Sai" is held on the Ping River. Thousands of small krathong made from coconut shells, with candles inside, are floated in long lines so they look like a river of light.
- Sukhothai: The festival is called "Loy Krathong Phao Thian Len Fai" (floating krathong, burning candles and playing with fire/light) and is held at Sukhothai Historical Park.
- Bangkok: People float krathong on the Chao Phraya River and in public park lakes such as Lumpini Park and Benjakitti Park.
- Northeast (Isan), Nakhon Phanom: The "Lai Ruea Fai" (illuminated boat procession) on the Mekong River is a related light festival, but it is held earlier, at the end of Buddhist Lent (Ok Phansa) in the 11th lunar month.
