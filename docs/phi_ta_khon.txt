PHI TA KHON (Ghost Festival of Dan Sai) | ประเพณีผีตาโขน ด่านซ้าย เลย

Overview
Phi Ta Khon is a festival in Dan Sai district, Loei province, in the Northeast of Thailand. It is part of a larger merit-making festival called "Bun Luang", which includes "Bun Phawet" (the merit-making for listening to the Vessantara Jataka) and "Bun Bang Fai" (the rocket festival).

Dates
There is no fixed date. The date is chosen each year by the local spirit medium, Chao Pho Kuan, through a ritual. It is usually held in June or July.

History and legend
The festival comes from the Vessantara Jataka, the story of the Buddha's last life before he became the Buddha. When Prince Vessantara came back to his city after a long exile, the celebration was so loud and happy that it woke up the spirits (phi) of the forest, and they followed him into the city. The people of Dan Sai dress as these spirits to remember the story.

Rituals and customs
- People wear large masks made from the base of a coconut palm leaf (for the face) and a sticky-rice steamer basket (huat) for the top, painted with bright colors and with a long nose.
- Costumes are colorful patchwork clothes. Phi Ta Khon "ghosts" often carry wooden swords or play with bells (mak kaleng) tied to their waist.
- Day 1: The ritual to invite Phra Uppakut (a protective spirit/monk) from the Man River to the temple, Wat Phon Chai.
- Day 2: Parade of Phi Ta Khon and the launching of bamboo rockets.
- Day 3: Listening to the 13 chapters of the Vessantara Jataka sermon at the temple.
- After the festival, masks are traditionally thrown into the river.
